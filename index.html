Plan the request

Sorting repairs from disclosure items before listing a property for sale

Before a listing, an owner may find several plumbing concerns: a stain, a slow drain, a heater on a rental contract, a valve that will not close. For an Etobicoke property, list each one, say whether it is active or a past event, and note any invoice, report or photo that dates it. A provider can then say which items call for repair now, and the rest can go to the person advising you on what a buyer should be told.

  • Active or past

    Mark each item as a current symptom or as something that happened before and was dealt with.

  • Dates and papers

    Gather the invoice, report or photo that shows when an item appeared and what was done about it.

  • Who handles disclosure

    Note who is helping you with the sale, because disclosure questions belong with them rather than with the provider.

Before you send

Photos That Help Before the First Call

Clear photos help a provider understand the job before anyone visits. Keep them handy for the follow-up call or email.

  • Water heater: the rating plate, the vent pipe where it leaves the tank, and the rental sticker or contract if there is one.
  • Blocked drain: which fixtures back up, whether it only happens on the lowest floor, and the floor drain or cleanout cap if you can find it.
  • Leak: the wet area or stain, the water meter with all fixtures turned off, and any recent work near the leak.
  • Sump pump: the pit with the lid off, where the discharge pipe leaves the house, and any battery or water-powered backup.
  • Older supply pipe: the pipe beside the water meter. Copper looks orange-brown, galvanized steel is grey and threaded, and lead is dull grey and turns shiny silver where you scratch it with a key.
FAQ

Questions About Your Etobicoke Request

Should I repair plumbing problems before listing my Etobicoke home?

That depends on the item. A fault that affects safe use, such as an active leak or a drain that backs up, may call for repair first, while ordinary wear may only need a clear record. Ask a provider to say which is which, and take disclosure questions to the representative or lawyer helping with the sale.

Who decides what a buyer must be told about past plumbing work?

Disclosure is a matter for you and the people advising you on the sale, such as a real estate representative or lawyer, not for the plumbing provider. The provider's role is to describe accurately what was found and what was done. Clear, dated records make that conversation easier and reduce the chance of an item being remembered wrongly.

What records should I collect before sending an Etobicoke plumbing request?

Gather invoices for earlier plumbing work, any inspection report, rental agreements for a heater or softener, and photos that show when a stain or problem first appeared. Note which items are current and which were dealt with before. Send what you have; a missing record is worth mentioning rather than guessing at.

Can several plumbing items be sent in one request before a sale?

Yes, if they are listed one by one with the room, the symptom and whether each is active or past. Say which items you hope to repair before listing and which you only want assessed and recorded. Connected items, such as a stain below a bathroom, are easier to judge together than apart.

What should a written record of plumbing work include for a sale?

It should be dated, name the address and the item, and describe the condition found, the work done and the parts used. Findings outside the agreed work should be listed separately. Keep the record with your other sale papers so it can be shown to a buyer's inspector if one asks about that item.

Is a camera inspection of the drain worth arranging before selling?

It can help when a drain has a history of backups and you want to know its condition rather than guess. Ask what the inspection would show, how the recording and a written summary would be provided, and how a finding would be worded, since a recorded finding becomes part of what you know about the house.

Once my pre-sale repair list is sent, has a provider for my Etobicoke address been found?

No. Sending a request does not book a visit or confirm that anyone covers your address. Our enquiry team reviews the details first. A provider who responds must then confirm their identity, qualifications, coverage, written scope, pricing and timing before any work is agreed, and you decide whether to go ahead.
