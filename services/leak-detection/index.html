Questions About the Work

Is this stain from an old leak or an active one?

An old mark left after a completed repair and a slow leak still feeding moisture can look alike, yet they call for different action before a sale.

  • Establish whether the area is still wet

    An active leak and an old mark call for different evidence, so ask how the respondent would tell them apart without opening the finish, for example by using the fixtures above it in turn and checking the area again. Give them the date the stain appeared, any photos taken since and the invoice from an earlier repair. Watching the area over a few days before the visit can add useful evidence.

  • Compare an opening with further observation

    Compare opening a ceiling or wall now with tracing the source first through observation and non-invasive checks. Ask which finishes would be disturbed, who restores them, and whether the evidence already justifies an opening before the listing.

  • Record the source, the repair and the patching

    Request a dated note naming the source found, the repair carried out and any ceiling or wall that was cut and patched. If the area was found dry and the mark is old, ask for that observation in writing too. If insulation, drywall or flooring in your Etobicoke home stayed wet, ask that this be noted on its own line with who would deal with it.
